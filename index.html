    Welcome

    ThePrototype is a collaborative 3D prototyping app that runs entirely in your browser — peer-to-peer, with no server and no accounts.

    You build scenes from primitives, imported models and packs, wire behavior with a visual node graph, and everything you do is instantly visible to everyone connected to you. Peers connect directly to each other (WebRTC); the only shared infrastructure is a small signaling service used to establish the connection.

    Quick start

    1. Open the app in a desktop browser (VR headset browsers work too).
    2. Create something — right-click the empty viewport and pick Add ▸ Mesh ▸ Cube. (If you prefer Shift+A for the Add menu, switch on Shift+A quick add in Settings ▸ Controls ▸ Keyboard & mouse.)
    3. Move it — click the cube to select it, then use the gizmo (1 move, 2 rotate, 3 scale).
    4. Invite a friend — open the Connect panel, copy your peer ID and send it to them. They paste it into their own Connect field and press Connect; you approve the request, and from then on you are editing the same scene together.

    Tip

    Everything replicates automatically: objects, transforms, materials, the node graph, chat, pings and voice. There is no "sync" button — if you can see it, your peers can too.

    Install it as an app. theprototype.app is a web app you can install: your browser's Install app (or Add to Home screen on a phone) puts it on your desktop or home screen, and it opens in its own window without the browser's bars — on a phone that gives the scene the whole height of the screen.

    Open source. The app itself is open source under the MIT licence: github.com/theprototype-app/core (also Settings ▸ About ▸ Source Code), with a contributing guide and an llms.txt for coding agents.

    What's new

    1.25 — your feedback, fixed: panels that keep their keys, water that behaves. Keys follow the panel you are in: WASD no longer fly the camera from the Explorer, the Inspector, a node's graph view, the HUD editor or any tool window, the panel with the keys shows an outline, the object list keeps Delete / F / Ctrl+D, and sliders in nodes never drag the graph. The code workspace gains sidebars — Open editors and a searchable Project tree (Ctrl+B); Outline, Problems, Bound nodes and Find in files (Ctrl+Alt+B, Ctrl+Shift+F) — plus a scrolling tab strip, Ctrl+P quick open, Ctrl+Shift+O go to symbol and a guard before closing unsaved code, and a game's Player has code you can open and save. The node editor opens with the whole graph in view, or where you left it (saved with the scene); Tidy graph (L, Shift+L) lays a graph out in one undo step, and every game's Main graph is tidied. A click now selects what is inside or behind water, Alt+click cycles everything under the cursor, and ping moved to Ctrl+Alt+click; opening a scene while another is loading always gives you the newest one. Things under water rise or sink by their density, every water setting works on every preset, scenes can start their simulation on load with one Reset / Pause for everything, and water gains visible bubble emitters, pour emitters, spilling fluid tanks and a lighter look on phones that still bends the light. New: Fluids — Add ▸ Water ▸ Fluid pours real particle water, Flow path makes rivers, chutes and pipes, the Rotate / Motor and Float Along Flow nodes turn wheels and float boats, and the Water works example runs a closed water loop. Also fixed: an old game scene with two modules no longer opens with its two Code link cards on top of each other, and particle fluid no longer tunnels through thin walls under pressure.

    1.24 — characters that walk, a race, and your games' stats. Everyone in a session is now a rigged character — nine CC0 KayKit adventurers and skeletons that walk, run, strafe and jump with their camera and reach for their VR controllers — and Customize Character (profile menu) is a side panel with a live preview on yourself: body, head (the character's own, a stylised one or your photo), hat, outfit colour and your ping; the classic floating head is one click away. Templates ▸ Games has a new game, Race: up to four players, three laps round a valley, with laps, speed and grip on the Race rules node and a road you reshape by moving its points. Edit Mesh gains slide edges, fill holes (F), connect (J) and dissolve vertices, solidify and separate, mitered bevel corners and rounded vertex bevels, and two meshes can be combined with a Boolean union, subtract or intersect from the object menu. Checkpoints (Ctrl+Shift+S) keep named and automatic versions of your scene on this device, with a timeline that restores, compares, notes and pins them, and Settings is a window with a section sidebar. Tools ▸ Recording… films a turntable or a flythrough as a webm, and Publish / Export ▸ Community gallery builds a gallery submission for GitHub. Every game keeps one permanent id, so re-exports and the play link count as one game in Your games & stats; community cards, play links and the pause menu get hearts, and the community browser a Mine filter. In a headset a game's HUD floats in front of you as laid out on the desktop, Tools ▸ AI opens an AI chat panel you can talk to, the assistant gets voice typing, and the VR keyboard and Settings panel gain punctuation and a Search tab. Modules can ask api.hud.vrHud() and give their HUD elements a vrText.

    1.23 — readable game logic. Every rebuilt game's rules are now one readable script on its Main graph — Mini Golf, Football, Dungeon Realms, The Alchemist's Escape, Sky Run, Target Toss and Marble Maze — wired to its buttons, HUD, sounds and the kit: tune a number in the node's properties panel, or change the code and press Ctrl+S, and the game plays by your rules on every player's screen, saved with the scene. The scene's graph is the Main graph, and opening an older game adds links to the code that runs it. Every node's properties are in the node editor's side panel (Script inputs, Behaviour params, module nodes), and a double-click opens any node's code — module code read-only, with Make editable copy for nodes bound to it. The code workspace puts scripts, behaviours, script files, module sources and the graph as JSON in tabs; Ctrl+S checks the code and reloads every node that runs it, on every peer, and broken code never applies. Script nodes grow the sockets their code uses and get api.object, raycast, keys and spawn; Math gains pow, sin, cos, abs, round, floor, clamp and negate; and HUD Button, On Game State and HUD Timer now give a value when wired into a number input. The node editor has its own keys — shortcuts follow the panel you clicked, so C opens the chat only from the 3D view — plus groups you open like folders, markdown notes and frames, right-click menus for the canvas, a node and a selection, mute, collapse, align and nudge, undo for every edit, and a ? cheat sheet. Settings ▸ Node types hides the node types you never use, groups save and load as .tpnode files, and the Edit Mesh keys can be rebound. For modules, api.kit.provide lends a game's rules its engine, behaviours get sockets, and api.flow.seedGraph seeds a wired example once.

    1.22 — VR + water. Any object can be water: Add ▸ Water makes a tank, a pool or an ocean with waves, refraction, caustics, foam, underwater fog and bubbles, in nine presets from Pool to Lava and Ice, plus Rain and Snow particle presets. Things float — or sink, or drift with a river — and Add ▸ Simulation ▸ Fluid tank is a glass box of liquid you can slosh and pour. The Jiggle node makes anything lag, wobble and sway. Concave scenery collides as it looks with exact mesh colliders, Decompose keeps a moving arch's openings, and collision groups make ghost walls; On Impact / On Enter / On Exit get only with and other. In a headset the radial menu has the desktop's names and icons, every VR setting is in it, every button can be remapped, and seated mode, height, smooth turning and the comfort vignette work everywhere, not only in games. Welcome to ThePrototype VR and a six-step editor tour show newcomers around (Tours), and the Quest browser offers Enter VR by itself. Opening a scene puts the camera on its start view at once — with Hold camera until loaded if you want it kept there — and Modern placeholders are the default. Community joins the profile menu under Support the project (Community), and Templates ▸ Examples has five new scenes: Aquarium, Pool party, Island ocean, Jelly room and Fluid tank toy.

    1.21 — quick fixes, publish anywhere. Menu ▸ Publish / Export gathers both ways out: publish to the community with a play link and QR code that open straight into the game, or export a zip of plain HTML that plays on itch.io or any static host, or an iframe snippet — every one with the Made with ThePrototype badge. On a phone, games get touch action buttons you can rearrange and re-skin. A new loading placeholder style fills up as each model downloads, can be moved while it waits, and turns red with Retry and Replace model… when a file fails. Every theme is readable now, Settings has a real search (names, groups and keywords), menus and panels no longer select text when you drag (setting), the logo menu fits and scrolls on short and folding phone screens, the Live profiler moved into the Profiler tab, a phone's automatic quality follows its own refresh rate, hand-set fog keeps its near and far, and Ctrl+D with nothing selected creates nothing.

    1.20 — five new games, measure on the device, build games from a kit. Five new games are in the Games tab with no download: The Alchemist's Escape, Marble Maze, Mini Golf, Sky Run and Target Toss. The Profiler records frame time, draw calls and triangles — and, in detail, which objects draw most — against the Quest budget, compares two recordings, and watches a headset live from the desktop; Report this moment keeps the last 30 seconds when something stutters. Games get a game kit (rules, rounds, levels, score, pickups, enemies with health and movers) as Kit: nodes and as code, and game logic can be written as a small behaviour file with a live node view. The Script node gains typed inputs and outputs, Flow Code shows a graph as compact text, and modules now unload completely.

    1.19 — doors that open, scenes that load, games that switch. Four new packs (Interiors, Town & Market, Interactive, Arcane Study) and two new example levels; doors, lids and levers work in Interact and Play, and nothing animates by itself any more. Every pack object has levels of detail. Big scenes load with a progress bar and Cancel, opening another scene asks about the game's modules, and Clear scene can clear the game setup too. A frame counter shows FPS and draw calls.

    1.18 — menus, levels and smooth frames in a headset. Every game has the same pause menu with per-game Settings and Levels; in VR the left X opens it. The controller laser is easier to see, the radial menu follows the thumbstick, teleport keeps you inside the play area, and some games let your grips move the world. Towers becomes twelve levels with stars, the Stars Room makes stars with a clap, and three new nodes — On Clap, Point Grab and Game Setting — build games out of settings and gestures. A headset starts with lighter quality and simplifies distant models.

    1.17 — games that look finished. Edit and Interact (the I key), click-through glass, a Module content section in the object list, and Test play that starts a game from its menu. Every game got a start screen, best scores saved on the device (Store Value), game sounds, music, banners and controller buzz; four kit packs and three walkable levels arrived.

    1.16 — Waves, and one world to keep. Waves joins the Games tab, the tab shows every game again, and two people pressing play at once no longer fight over the simulation.

    1.15 — where everyone is. Every scene card in the Explorer shows who is in it, with Join ‹name›; scenes can be renamed and their files follow; copy and paste a scene to get a new one. Dungeon Realms and Untangle became games, ?embed=1 hides the editor for embedding, and (1.15.1) public rooms take a code or a knock.

    1.14 — heavy models stop being a cliff. A model is weighed before it lands, and one that would hurt asks first, with Reduce on a background worker as a third way out and Restore original model to take it back. See Importing files. Football joins Towers and the Stars Room as a Games-tab template.

    1.13 — knock it about. Hit a floating object with your hand and it flies off, with an On Hit node to react to it; the Stars Room and VR Football are built on it. Scene look becomes one section with post-processing node graphs, materials can be shared between duplicates, and proportional editing reaches your peers.

    1.12 — hold together. A connection request now ends rather than hanging, a session has a size, the signaling link stops giving up, and a scene too heavy for your machine reduces quality or pauses instead of freezing — with a Statistics panel and a diagnostics bundle you can copy into a bug report.

    Building

    • Explorer — your local asset library: import files, organize folders, drag assets into the scene.
    • Packs — ready-made model and audio collections you can browse and import.
    • Prefabs — save any object as a reusable asset.
    • Mesh Editing — vertices, edges and faces: extrude, bevel, knife, loops and mirroring.
    • Snapping — line things up: grid steps, surfaces, and snapping onto real geometry.
    • UV & Textures — unwrap a model, paint on it, and give parts of it their own materials.
    • Scene Look (Post-processing) — grade the finished frame: ambient occlusion, colour, bloom, grain. Saved with the scene and shared with everyone.
    • Animation — keyframe clips with a timeline, curves, markers and onion skin.
    • Physics & Simulation — mass, joints, dropping and throwing objects.
    • Colliders — collider shapes, exact meshes, decomposition, collision groups and sensors.
    • Particle Effects — dust, smoke, fire, sparkles, rain and snow.
    • Water — tanks, pools, oceans and lava with waves, refraction, caustics, foam and bubbles.
    • Fluid Tank & Jiggle — particle liquid you can pour, and springy secondary motion.
    • Fluids — particle water poured into the scene, rivers, chutes and pipes, and wheels that turn it.
    • Terrain & Sculpting — add ground and shape it with a brush.
    • Saving & Sessions — the .tpscene bundle format, GLTF export, sessions and autosave.

    The scene

    • Music & Sound — shared background music, spatial sound and voice chat.

    Behavior, AI & more

    • Community — publish a scene from the app, share its link, play and remix what others made, enter a contest.
